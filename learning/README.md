# Daily Learning System

Created: 2026-06-13

## Goal

Spend about 1 focused hour per day learning technical material that is both intrinsically interesting and likely to compound professionally.

The aim is not immediate utility. The aim is to keep curiosity alive while steadily building a deeper theory of agentic systems, training, and world models.

## Long-Run Stance

This is not a sprint plan.

Many people treat life as a race that is already near its ending: optimize every choice, avoid anything without immediate payoff, and move with a constant sense of scarcity. This learning system starts from the opposite assumption: for both AI and personal life, things are still early.

The goal is to live and learn like a long-distance runner:

- Keep a sustainable daily pace instead of chasing intensity.
- Learn some things for their intrinsic beauty and not only for utility.
- Let curiosity produce professional compounding, but do not reduce curiosity to ROI.
- Avoid urgency that comes from social comparison.
- Treat each month as another lap in a long arc, not as a final verdict on capability or status.

The plan should therefore protect aliveness, attention, and depth. If a learning schedule makes life feel narrower, more anxious, or more performative, it is failing even if the material is technically impressive.

## North Star

Build a personal theory of verifiable intelligent systems:

- Agent harness / auto-research: how an agent should perceive, act, evaluate itself, explain failure, and improve through a closed loop.
- Training: how model capability is actually produced, scaled, optimized, and made economical.
- World models: how systems form predictive internal state, plan through imagined consequences, and connect perception to action.

The professional compounding thesis is that these three areas meet at the same place: reliable agents require better evaluation loops, better training/data flywheels, and better internal models of environment dynamics.

## Default Daily Contract

- 45 minutes: read one bounded unit, such as one chapter, one paper section, or one lecture note.
- 10 minutes: write notes in your own words.
- 5 minutes: record one follow-up question, one usable concept, or one disagreement.

If the day is overloaded, the minimum viable version is 20 minutes reading plus 3 bullets in `reading-log.md`.

## Material States

- Current pool: material already chosen for active reading.
- Recommendation pool: curated candidates, not commitments.
- Parking lot: interesting but not worth attention this month.

Avoid turning the recommendation pool into obligation. Every weekly update should move at most 1-2 items into the current pool.

## Selection Rules

Prefer material that satisfies at least two of these:

- It sharpens your existing agent harness theory.
- It helps explain observed agent failure modes at work.
- It improves understanding of training, scaling, data, evaluation, or inference economics.
- It gives a reusable abstraction rather than only a tool tutorial.
- It is close enough to your work that examples can be tested against real trajectories or benchmarks.

Deprioritize:

- Generic agent framework tutorials.
- Papers that only rename planning / memory / tool use without measurable mechanism.
- Long reading sequences where the output is only "I know more" and not a clearer model, benchmark, or experiment.

## Weekly Update Loop

Every week, update:

1. What did I actually read?
2. What changed in my view?
3. What should move from recommendation pool to current pool?
4. What should be deleted or parked?
5. What assumption, experiment, or principle should be created in the broader life system?
